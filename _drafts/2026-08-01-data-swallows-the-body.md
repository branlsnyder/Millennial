Coffee, Pour Over Equipment, and Electronics

Brandon brews coffee each morning. Brandon says it’s a way to unplug, occupy his hands with something tactile, and not spend too much time on his phone or computer. 

Unfortunately, Brandon has already been completely devoured, digested, and metabolized by the devices and screens that he’s invited into his life. 

The grind size (medium fine), coffee-to-water ratio (1:16), steep and pouring time (50 grams of water every 30 seconds), roast (light), and varietal (Indonesian anaerobic) are all constantly monitored through specialized kettles, scales, and timers. He daily iterates on this brewing ritual, tasting and refining each step, similar to how an algorithm extracts engagement data from  the likes, follows, and comments of its users. His physical activities have been assimilated to the culture of his digital experiences.
And all for the consumption of  coffee, a caffeinated, low-cal beverage drunk for the purposes of ‘locking in’ and ‘winning the day’, checking off daily tasks set the night before on his Notion project management app. His coffee, a source of daily leisure, has been folded into the logic of optimized productivity.

Brandon (data) swallows (audibly, on the microphone) the coffee (body).
